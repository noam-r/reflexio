# Expectation Confidence, Importance, and Rigidity

This page defines the main modifiers that determine how strongly an expectation influences emotional processing.

## Purpose

Explain why not all expectations matter equally, even when they concern the same outcome.

## Core Idea

An expectation does not have emotional force only because of its content. Its impact also depends on how strongly it is held, how much it matters, and how flexible it is. The architecture therefore treats confidence, importance, and rigidity as core modifiers of expectation strength.

These modifiers help explain why one unmet expectation may barely register while another creates a major emotional shift.

## Expectation Confidence

Expectation confidence describes how certain the system is that an expectation is correct.

A high-confidence expectation creates a stronger basis for discrepancy when reality differs from it. If the system was almost sure something would happen and it does not, the event can produce a strong predictive shock or destabilization.

Confidence helps explain:
- surprise
- expectation collapse
- certainty-based disappointment
- calibration errors

## Expectation Importance

Expectation importance describes how meaningful the expected outcome is to the system.

Two expectations can have equal confidence but very different emotional weight. A minor expectation may have little impact when unmet. A high-importance expectation may strongly affect self-regard, trust, fairness, or relational meaning.

Importance helps explain:
- depth of emotional impact
- why some events feel central rather than incidental
- why a small event can matter a great deal in a significant relationship

## Expectation Rigidity

Expectation rigidity describes how tightly the system is attached to the expectation and how hard it is to tolerate deviation.

A rigid expectation is less able to bend under ambiguity. This often increases the emotional consequences of discrepancy because the system has fewer internal pathways for absorbing difference without distress or destabilization.

Rigidity helps explain:
- overreaction to ambiguity
- difficulty reinterpreting events
- persistence of emotional disruption
- resistance to reflective adjustment

## Why These Modifiers Matter Together

The emotional effect of an unmet expectation often depends on all three:
- confidence determines how certain the system was
- importance determines how much it mattered
- rigidity determines how hard it is to absorb the mismatch

A highly confident, highly important, and highly rigid expectation can make even a small event deeply significant.

## Example

If the system strongly expects laughter, deeply values that response, and treats the expectation rigidly, a lack of laughter may produce embarrassment, hurt, or anger. If the same expectation is weak, low in importance, and flexible, the same outcome may barely matter.

## Design Rule

Expectation modifiers should explain why an outcome mattered as much as it did, not merely whether it matched or mismatched the expected result.
