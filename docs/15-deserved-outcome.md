# Deserved Outcome

This page defines deserved outcome as what the system believes should happen.

## Purpose

Explain the norm-based side of expectation and why fairness, respect, reciprocity, and entitlement must be modeled explicitly.

## Core Idea

A deserved outcome is the result the system believes should happen. It answers the question:

> What ought to happen here?

This is different from what the system predicts and different from what it wants. A deserved outcome represents a normative expectation grounded in fairness, acknowledgment, reciprocity, respect, role, effort, or relational obligation.

## Why Deserved Outcome Matters

Normative expectations often drive some of the strongest emotional reactions. The system may correctly predict a poor outcome and may not even strongly want a better one, yet still feel hurt or angry because it believes a better outcome should have been given.

This is why deserved outcome is crucial for explaining:
- anger
- resentment
- indignation
- humiliation
- unfairness
- moral disappointment

## Deserved Outcome vs Predictive Expectation

These must remain separate.

- predictive expectation asks what is likely
- deserved outcome asks what should happen

A counterparty may be predicted to ignore the system. If the counterparty does ignore it, surprise may be low, but a strong normative discrepancy may remain if the system believed acknowledgment should have happened.

## Deserved Outcome vs Desired Outcome

These must also remain separate.

- desired outcome asks what is wanted
- deserved outcome asks what is believed to be owed, proper, or fair

The system may want approval without believing approval is deserved. It may believe respect is deserved even if it did not especially desire closeness. This distinction gives the architecture more moral and relational precision.

## What Deserved Outcome Influences

Deserved outcome strongly affects:
- anger
- resentment
- hurt dignity
- fairness judgment
- perceived disrespect
- moral repair and apology processing

It is especially important when the event is experienced as not merely unpleasant, but wrong.

## Example

If the system believes a counterparty should at least acknowledge a sincere effort, and the counterparty dismisses it, the emotional reaction may include anger or injured self-regard even if the system had predicted the dismissal. The intensity comes from the violation of what should have happened.

## Design Rule

A deserved outcome should represent a normative claim about the event, not a wish and not a forecast.
