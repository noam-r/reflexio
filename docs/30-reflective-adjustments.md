# Reflective Adjustments

This page explains bounded changes that can improve processing without denying the original event.

## Purpose

Define the controlled changes the system can propose after reflective review.

## Core Idea

A reflective adjustment is a bounded change to expectation, confidence, interpretation, attribution, or scope that supports more adaptive processing of an event. It does not deny what happened. Instead, it asks whether the internal chain that processed the event can be made more accurate, proportional, or less damaging.

Reflective adjustments are one of the architecture’s main tools for growth.

## Why Reflective Adjustments Matter

Some emotional outcomes become harmful not because the event itself was unreal, but because the processing chain was too rigid, too global, too certain, or too self-damaging.

Reflective adjustments allow the system to improve that chain while preserving the original facts.

## Common Adjustment Targets

Reflective adjustments may focus on:
- expectation strength
- confidence in the expectation
- rigidity of interpretation
- attribution of intent
- scope of self-impact
- coupling to self-regard
- tolerance for ambiguity
- perceived deservedness

These are not arbitrary edits. They are specific points where the original processing may have become distorted or overly damaging.

## What Makes an Adjustment Valid

A good reflective adjustment should be:
- reality-compatible
- proportional
- traceable
- specific
- less distorted than the previous interpretation

The goal is not to force positivity. The goal is to improve processing quality.

## Example

If the system interpreted silence after a joke as “I am uninteresting,” a reflective adjustment may narrow the scope:
- from “this says something global about me”
- to “this moment did not land as hoped”

This can reduce shame without denying disappointment.

## Adjustment vs Denial

Reflective adjustment must not become denial. It should not say:
- nothing happened
- the event did not matter
- the system should not have felt anything

Instead, it should say:
- the reaction made sense given the prior processing chain
- parts of that chain may be revised
- the event can now be understood in a healthier or more proportional way

## Results of Reflective Adjustment

A successful reflective adjustment may lead to:
- reduced unnecessary self-damage
- clearer boundaries
- better expectation calibration
- softer overgeneralization
- improved ambiguity tolerance
- more adaptive future processing

It may also generate new ledger entries if the adjustment changes current emotional meaning.

## Design Rule

Reflective adjustments should change the processing path, not falsify the event.
