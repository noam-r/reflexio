# Relationship Lens

This page explains how the current relationship state changes the interpretation of past events.

## Purpose

Define the relational frame through which counterparties and earlier events are understood in the present.

## Core Idea

The relationship lens is the current relational frame through which the system interprets a specific counterparty and reinterprets past events involving that counterparty. It is not a separate memory record. It is the present configuration of the relationship state as it functions during interpretation.

This matters because the same event can mean something different when viewed from a changed relationship.

## Why the Relationship Lens Matters

An event is not interpreted in isolation. Its meaning depends partly on:
- who it came from
- what the relationship was at the time
- what the relationship is now
- whether the relationship has deepened, weakened, repaired, or destabilized

As the relationship changes, the lens changes. This means old events can be re-read without changing the original facts.

## Examples of Lens Change

A blunt remark may first feel insulting when trust is low. Later, after warmth and familiarity grow, the same remark may be understood as teasing or informality.

The opposite can also happen. A flattering comment may once seem kind, but later, after repeated manipulation, it may be reinterpreted as strategic or insincere.

The relationship lens therefore supports both softening and darkening reinterpretation.

## What Shapes the Lens

The relationship lens is shaped by the current relationship state, including factors such as:
- trust
- familiarity
- warmth
- caution
- salience
- perceived sincerity
- repairability
- attachment

These variables influence what interpretations feel plausible in the present.

## Relationship Lens vs Original Context

The architecture should preserve both:
- the relationship context that existed when the event first occurred
- the relationship lens through which the event is replayed now

This distinction is essential for explainability. It allows the system to say:
- “I saw this one way then”
- “I see it differently now because the relationship changed”

## Why This Improves Reflection

The relationship lens helps the system explain why old meanings shift over time. It also prevents reinterpretation from feeling arbitrary, because the new interpretation can be linked to a concrete relational change.

## Example

Suppose the system once interpreted silence after a joke as rejection. Months later, after a strong pattern of care and playfulness develops with the same counterparty, replay through the new relationship lens may support a softer interpretation: the counterparty may simply have missed the moment.

The event stays the same. The lens changes.

## Design Rule

A relationship lens should explain present interpretation through current relationship state, not replace the original historical context.
