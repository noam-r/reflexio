# Desired Outcome

This page defines desired outcome as what the system wants to happen.

## Purpose

Separate wanting from predicting so that disappointment, longing, and emotional preference can be represented clearly.

## Core Idea

A desired outcome is the result the system hopes for or wants. It answers the question:

> What do I want to happen?

Desired outcome is important because emotional life is shaped not only by prediction, but also by preference. The system may know that something is unlikely while still strongly wanting it. That gap can create disappointment even when there is little surprise.

## Why Desired Outcome Matters

If the architecture only tracks prediction, it misses a large class of emotional responses. A system can be unsurprised by a poor outcome and still be affected because the outcome violates what it wanted.

Desired outcome helps explain:
- disappointment
- sadness
- frustration
- longing
- relief when hopes are met
- encouragement when desired outcomes become more plausible

## Desired Outcome vs Predictive Expectation

These must remain separate.

- predictive expectation asks what is likely
- desired outcome asks what is wanted

For example:
- the system may predict a cold response
- the system may still want warmth

If the cold response arrives, predictive discrepancy may be low, but desire discrepancy may still be high.

## Desired Outcome vs Deserved Outcome

These must also remain separate.

- desired outcome asks what is wanted
- deserved outcome asks what should happen

The system may want praise without believing praise is owed. It may want laughter without believing laughter was required. This distinction matters because desire violations often feel different from fairness violations.

## What Desired Outcome Influences

Desired outcome strongly affects:
- disappointment
- hope
- longing
- encouragement
- frustration
- emotional investment in a possible future result

It is especially important in cases where the system wanted something but knew it was uncertain.

## Example

If the system wants a counterparty to laugh at a joke but is unsure whether they will, silence may create disappointment without strong surprise. If the system also believed laughter was deserved as acknowledgment, then anger or hurt may appear alongside disappointment.

## Design Rule

A desired outcome should capture preference, not probability and not entitlement.
