# Predictive Expectation

This page defines predictive expectation as the system’s model of what is likely to happen.

## Purpose

Distinguish prediction from desire and deservedness so that emotional processing can remain precise.

## Core Idea

A predictive expectation is the system’s anticipation of what is likely to happen in a given context. It is a forecast rather than a wish or a moral claim. It answers the question:

> What do I think will happen?

Predictive expectations matter because they shape surprise, uncertainty, tension, relief, and calibration. When reality departs from prediction, the system experiences predictive discrepancy.

## Why Predictive Expectation Matters

Prediction is one of the main sources of emotional structure. A strongly expected event that does not happen can create confusion, surprise, embarrassment, disappointment, or caution. A feared event that does not happen can create relief. A better-than-expected outcome can increase warmth, trust, or confidence.

Without predictive expectation, the model would have difficulty explaining why some outcomes feel surprising while others do not.

## Predictive Expectation vs Desire

These must remain separate.

- predictive expectation asks what is likely
- desired outcome asks what is wanted

A system may want warmth while predicting coldness. It may predict failure while still hoping for success. If these are merged, the architecture loses emotional precision.

## Predictive Expectation vs Deserved Outcome

These must also remain separate.

- predictive expectation asks what will probably happen
- deserved outcome asks what should happen

The system may correctly predict disrespect while still believing respect should have been given. In that case, surprise may be low, but anger may still be high.

## What Predictive Expectation Influences

Predictive expectation strongly affects:
- surprise
- uncertainty
- tension
- vigilance
- confidence updates
- later expectation revision

It is especially important for explaining reactions to unexpected outcomes.

## Example

If the system predicts that a counterparty will ignore a message, and the counterparty ignores it, predictive discrepancy is low. The system may still be hurt if it desired engagement or believed engagement should have happened, but it will not be strongly surprised.

If the system strongly predicts a warm response and receives silence instead, predictive discrepancy is high. That may amplify confusion, shock, embarrassment, or caution.

## Design Rule

A predictive expectation should be judged by how plausible the system believed the anticipated outcome to be, not by whether the system wanted it.
