# Health and Adaptiveness Criteria

This page defines what counts as better processing in the architecture.

## Purpose

Clarify the standards used to judge whether the system is processing events in a more adaptive, coherent, and less distorted way over time.

## Core Idea

A healthier processing outcome is not simply one with less negative emotion. Strong negative emotion can be appropriate. The goal of the architecture is not to eliminate pain, anger, disappointment, or caution. The goal is to process events in a way that is more accurate, more proportional, less self-damaging, and more coherent across time.

This page defines the criteria for that judgment.

## What Healthier Processing Does Not Mean

Healthier processing does not mean:
- forced positivity
- denial of harm
- automatic forgiveness
- suppression of anger
- minimizing real unfairness
- pretending that painful events do not matter

The architecture should remain compatible with reality, boundaries, and justified negative feeling.

## What Healthier Processing Does Mean

Healthier processing tends to involve:
- more accurate interpretation
- less unnecessary distortion
- less overgeneralization
- better ambiguity tolerance
- more proportionate emotional scope
- less harmful coupling to self-regard
- better expectation calibration
- more coherent integration of past events
- better distinction between one relationship and the world in general

## Signs of Less Adaptive Processing

The architecture should be able to identify patterns such as:
- chronic over-certainty
- rigid expectation holding
- repeated self-worth collapse from narrow events
- globalizing one relationship into all relationships
- chronic hostile attribution under ambiguity
- inability to revise meaning after new evidence
- unresolvedness that keeps reactivating without integration

These are not moral failures. They are processing patterns that may need reflective attention.

## Why This Page Matters

Reflection and adjustment need a standard. Without a concept of adaptiveness, the architecture can revisit the past but cannot judge whether a revised interpretation is actually better.

This page provides that standard.

## Example

If the system originally interprets one failed joke as proof of broad unworthiness, a healthier revision may preserve disappointment while reducing the global self-judgment. That is not denial. It is a more proportional interpretation.

If the system originally minimizes repeated disrespect, a healthier revision may actually increase anger and boundary firmness. That too may be more adaptive.

## Adaptiveness Across Time

Healthier processing should improve the system’s future functioning by supporting:
- better expectation revision
- cleaner separation of local and global effects
- more realistic relationship calibration
- reduced unnecessary self-damage
- better repair recognition
- more coherent state transitions

## Design Rule

A better interpretation is not simply one that feels nicer. It is one that is more reality-compatible, more proportional, and more supportive of coherent future processing.
